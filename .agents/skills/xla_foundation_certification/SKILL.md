# Foundation of XM and XLA® Certification

Use this knowledge base when advising the user on Experience Level Agreements (XLA), Experience Management (XM), or helping them prepare for the Foundation certification exam.

## Exam Details
- **Provider**: XLA Institute and Van Haren Certify.
- **Format**: 40 multiple-choice questions (Online, Open Book, English).
- **Time Limit**: 60 minutes.
- **Passing Grade**: 80% (32 correct out of 40).
- **Taxonomy**: Tests at Bloom Level 1 (Recall & Retention) and Bloom Level 2 (Understanding).
- **Note**: Exam contains negative questions (e.g., "which is NOT a principle of...").

## The XM Journey (The 5 Ds)
An approach for embedding Experience Management into an operating model to improve collaboration, experience, and business impact. The phases do not need to be strictly sequential.

1. **Define**: Determines the scope of the journey. Includes Commitments, Purpose, Positioning, and BHAG (Big Hairy Audacious Goal).
2. **Discover**: Clarifies the existing baseline situation. Includes assessing Experience (Persona, Customer Journey Map, Service Blueprint), Collaboration (Stakeholder Map), and Business Impact.
3. **Dream**: Imagines what could be done given the existing situation and BHAG. Includes setting Ambition (Organization & Service), Gap Analysis, and Roadmap.
4. **Design**: Focuses on creating or improving collaboration and services in co-creation. Includes Prototyping, Method (O-data/X-data collection, reverse brainstorming), XLA formulation, and setting up an Experience Management Office (XMO).
5. **Deliver**: Delivering, monitoring, and reporting better experiences. Includes the Taskforce, Governance, Processes, Tooling, and Reporting.

## Key Terminology & Concepts
- **SLA (Service Level Agreement)**: A documented agreement identifying required services and expected levels. Often output-focused (e.g., uptime, latency).
- **XLA (Xperience Level Agreement)**: An outcome-oriented, measurable agreement concerning collaboration, experience, and business impact. Overcomes the limitations of SLAs.
- **XM (Experience Management)**: A data-driven approach combining operational measurements (O-data) and sentiment measurements (X-data) to correlate output with human-focused outcome.
- **XLA Value Drivers**: Experience, Collaboration, and Business Impact.
- **O-data**: Objective operational data (e.g., system latency, availability).
- **X-data (XIs)**: Subjective human perception data (e.g., satisfaction, feelings).
- **Magic Zone**: The correlation of X-data (XLA) with O-data (SLA) providing actionable insights.
- **Watermelon Syndrome**: When service providers show "green" KPIs (system is up), but customer emotions are "red" (negative experience). 
- **Peak-End Rule**: People remember the most intense moment (peak) and the last moment (end) of an experience better than the rest.
- **Jobs to be Done (JTBD)**: What someone wants to achieve using a product/service. Framework: *When I [situation], I want [motivation], so that [expected outcome].*
- **Gravity of Expectations**: The experience of a company's performance naturally declines over time if there are no improvements.
- **Moonshot Thinking**: Seeking massive improvements in perception at a fraction of the cost of equivalent technical improvements.
- **Experience Economy**: An economy where the *experience* determines the economic value, following the agrarian, industrial, and service economies.
- **Task Force**: A temporary team for rapid XM adoption.
- **XMO (Experience Management Office)**: A permanent function responsible for ongoing XM practices once it becomes business-as-usual.
- **Zone of Good Enough**: The range of acceptable quality levels that prevent terrible experiences while renouncing unnecessarily delightful ones.
